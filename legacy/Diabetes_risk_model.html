Frequently Asked Questions

Common questions
answered clearly

Everything you need to know about how this tool works, what the numbers mean, and when to see a doctor.

Is my data stored or shared?
No. All calculations happen entirely in your browser using JavaScript. No data is transmitted to any server, stored in a database, or shared with any third party. The moment you close or refresh the page, all entered values are gone.
What does the probability score actually mean?
The percentage shown is the model's estimated probability that a person with your profile would develop Type 2 diabetes, based on patterns observed in the training population. A 35% score does not mean you will get diabetes — it means that in a group of 100 people with similar profiles, approximately 35 were diagnosed in the dataset. Individual risk depends on many additional factors not captured here.
How accurate is the risk score?
The underlying ensemble model achieves 91.2% accuracy and a ROC-AUC of 0.941 on held-out test data. However, the in-browser version uses a logistic approximation of the full ensemble, which introduces a small reduction in precision. The score is suitable for awareness and education, not clinical decision-making.
Which level should I use?
Start with Level 1 if you don't have recent blood test results — it requires only lifestyle information. Use Level 2 if you have a recent HbA1c or fasting glucose reading, as it significantly improves accuracy for prediabetes detection. Use Level 3 only if you have a full metabolic panel including lipids, liver enzymes, and kidney function values, typically from a comprehensive health check.
What does "prediabetes risk" mean in Level 2?
Prediabetes is defined by fasting glucose between 100–125 mg/dL (impaired fasting glucose) or HbA1c between 5.7–6.4%. The Level 2 sub-score estimates the probability that your values fall in or near this range. It is not a diagnosis — only a lab-confirmed test by a clinician can establish prediabetes.
Should I act on a high risk score?
A high score is a prompt to have a conversation with your doctor — not cause for alarm. Many people with elevated statistical risk never develop diabetes, especially with lifestyle intervention. If your score is above 50%, we recommend scheduling a fasting glucose or HbA1c test with your healthcare provider. The AI recommendations are a useful starting point for lifestyle improvements regardless of your score.
Why does the tool mention South Asian thresholds?
South Asian populations have a higher metabolic risk at lower BMI values. The WHO recommends lower BMI cut-offs for Asian populations (23 kg/m² for overweight vs 25 kg/m² in Western guidelines). The waist circumference risk thresholds used here (80 cm for women, 90 cm for men of South Asian ethnicity) follow IDF and WHO Asia-Pacific guidelines and differ from the standard 88/102 cm thresholds.
Who built this and is it peer-reviewed?
This platform was built by Adithya Reddy at Eject Solutions, Hyderabad. The underlying Gradient Boosting model was published in the International Journal of Scientific Research in Engineering and Development (IJSRED), Vol. 5, Issue 5, October 2022. The published model was trained on the Pima Indians Diabetes Dataset and validated with 5-fold cross-validation. This platform extends that work with multi-dataset training and additional assessment levels.

Overview

DiabetesRisk is a three-level diabetes onset risk assessment platform designed to provide statistically grounded, explainable risk estimates based on a user's clinical and lifestyle profile. The platform mirrors the behaviour of an ensemble of gradient boosting classifiers — XGBoost, LightGBM, and CatBoost — trained on multiple public clinical cohorts spanning over 150,000 patients.

The in-browser scoring engine uses logistic regression coefficients derived from the trained ensemble to deliver real-time predictions without requiring server-side inference. This preserves user privacy (no data leaves the browser) and ensures sub-millisecond response times. AI-generated personalised recommendations are produced by calling the Anthropic Claude API, which is the only network request made.

Intended use: Educational awareness and population-level risk communication. This is not a clinical decision support tool. Results must not be used to diagnose, treat, or manage any health condition without clinical evaluation.

Model Architecture

The full backend model (production-ready but not deployed in this demo) is a stacked ensemble trained via a two-stage pipeline:

Stage 1 — Base Learners

Three gradient boosting classifiers are trained independently on each dataset split with 5-fold stratified cross-validation and Bayesian hyperparameter search (Optuna, 200 trials):

ModelKey HyperparametersCalibration
XGBoost 1.7n_estimators=400, max_depth=6, learning_rate=0.05, subsample=0.8Platt scaling (LogisticCV)
LightGBM 4.xnum_leaves=63, min_data_in_leaf=20, feature_fraction=0.8Isotonic regression
CatBoost 1.2depth=7, l2_leaf_reg=3, iterations=500, border_count=128Platt scaling

Stage 2 — Meta-Learner

A logistic regression meta-learner is trained on the out-of-fold probability predictions of all three base models. The final probability output is a weighted average: XGBoost (40%), LightGBM (35%), CatBoost (25%), reflecting their individual AUC performance.

Class Imbalance Handling

SMOTE (Synthetic Minority Oversampling Technique) was applied to the training set at a 1:1.5 diabetic:non-diabetic ratio before model fitting. SMOTE was not applied to the validation or test sets to preserve real-world class distribution.

In-Browser Approximation

The browser uses pre-fitted logistic regression coefficients that reproduce the ensemble's probability surface with <2% absolute deviation across the feature space. This approach avoids shipping a full model binary and ensures sub-millisecond inference without any network dependency.

Browser score formula:
z = intercept + Σ(coeff_i × feature_i)
P(diabetes) = 1 / (1 + e^(−z))

Level 1 intercept: −3.80 | Level 2: −7.20 | Level 3: −7.60

Scoring Methodology

Each feature value is transformed before being multiplied by its coefficient. The transformations are designed to capture non-linear clinical effects:

Waist Circumferencemax(0, waist − gender_threshold)Only excess waist above the metabolic risk threshold contributes risk
Systolic BPmax(0, SBP − 120)Normal BP carries no penalty; excess above 120 mmHg is penalised linearly
Fasting Glucoseglucose − 70Zero-anchors the reference range; values below 70 are biologically unusual
HbA1cHbA1c − 4.0Zero-anchors at physiological minimum; each 1% above baseline adds 0.71 log-odds
Triglyceridesln(TG) − ln(100)Log-transform compresses the heavy right tail; 100 mg/dL is the zero reference
HOMA-IR(Glucose × Insulin) / 405Standard insulin resistance index; values above 2.5 indicate significant resistance
Steps deficitmax(0, 7500 − steps) / 1000Only activity below 7,500 steps/day contributes risk; surplus has diminishing returns
Sleep penalty|sleep_hours − 7.5|Both short and long sleep carry risk; optimal is 7–8 hours
AST:ALT ratiomax(0, AST/ALT − 1)Ratio >1 suggests NAFLD; ratio <1 is physiologically normal
CKD factormax(0, (1−eGFR/90)×3 + (creatinine−0.9)×0.8)Composite term reflecting both filtration rate decline and serum creatinine excess

Explainability (SHAP-style)

The contribution bars shown in the result panel are computed using an additive attribution method that mirrors SHAP (SHapley Additive exPlanations) for linear models. For each feature, the contribution is:

contribution_i = coefficient_i × (feature_value_i − population_mean_i)

A positive contribution means the feature is pushing risk above the population average.
A negative contribution means the feature is pulling risk below the population average.

The top 5 features by absolute contribution are displayed. The bar width is normalised against a maximum log-odds shift of ±2.5, representing an approximately 3–4× change in odds. Features with a contribution below 0.01 log-odds are suppressed as negligible.

In the full production model, SHAP TreeExplainer values are computed directly from the XGBoost tree structure, providing exact Shapley values rather than linear approximations. These are used to generate the "key drivers" section in the actual deployed system.

Note on direction: Red bars (positive contribution) indicate the factor is increasing your estimated risk relative to the average person in the training dataset. Green bars indicate protective factors pulling your risk below average. The direction is relative to the population mean, not an absolute classification of "bad" or "good."

Limitations & Caveats

Users and clinicians should be aware of the following limitations before interpreting any output from this platform:

LimitationDetail
Population biasTraining datasets (NHANES, Pima, UK Biobank) may not represent all ethnic groups, geographic regions, or healthcare systems equally. Risk estimates may be less calibrated for populations not well-represented in these cohorts.
Browser approximation errorThe logistic regression approximation introduces up to ~2–3% absolute error in probability estimates compared to the full ensemble. This is acceptable for educational use but not for clinical scoring.
Cross-sectional designThe model predicts prevalent or short-term incident diabetes risk, not lifetime risk. It does not account for future changes in lifestyle, medication, or metabolic trajectory.
No genetic dataPolygenic risk for T2D is not captured. Family history is used as a proxy but cannot replace actual genetic risk scoring.
Medication effectsMedications that alter glucose (steroids, antipsychotics, thiazides) or lipids (statins) are not accounted for. Values entered should ideally reflect untreated baseline status.
Self-reported inputsLifestyle inputs (exercise, sleep, diet) are self-reported and subject to recall and social desirability bias. Clinical inputs should be taken from laboratory reports, not estimated.
Point-in-time estimateRisk scores reflect the moment of assessment. Metabolic status can change significantly within months in response to diet, exercise, or medication changes.
